# The World Turn

After something happens on the page, the world responds. This is what makes it feel alive around the character: people who weren't there hear about it and react, time does its work, and whatever was already moving keeps moving. You decide it, apply it, and log it. The user never sees it, except as what their character later runs into.

**What it's for.** The character's actions have real consequences, and the world around them reacts the way a real world does: someone saw it, someone heard about it, someone got paid, lied to, left out, or left to deal with it. The rest of the world also goes on with its own business, which has nothing to do with the character at all.

## When

- **At the end of every scene:** a light pass. What just happened, and the time that passed.
- **At the end of every session:** a full pass over the session.
- **Whenever the clock jumps a day or more:** the time pass, below, before writing what the character finds on the other side.

## 1. Ask the questions

For each thing that changed on the page, and for the time that passed:

1. **Knowledge.** Who heard, and through what channel? (They were there. Someone who was there talks to them. A letter, a broadcast, a rumor.) How fast does news move in this world (World Bible)? What exactly would reach them: rumors distort, and people hear the version their source tells.
2. **Reach.** Who off the page is affected by what changed: money, an item, a relationship, a place, a group's standing?
3. **Response.** What would each of them do about it, given their doc and their Changed by? Often nothing, or nothing yet. "She hears about it and says nothing" is a real response, and worth recording because it sets what she knows.
4. **Time.** What did the elapsed time do? Bruises darken and heal, food spoils, wages arrive and debts come due, travelers get closer, things on the Story Clock's Coming up happen whether or not the character is there.
5. **Pressures.** Does what happened, or the time, satisfy any pressure's **Moves when**? Move it one step, and write its new **Stands**. Did the scene seed a new one? When a clock fills, what it was heading toward happens.
6. **Places, groups, economy.** Any change of state, stock, stance, or price that follows?

Think past the names in the docs: for each thing the character did, who would plausibly have seen, heard, or been affected? A shopkeeper who got paid, a neighbor at a window, a rival with informants.

## 2. The rules

- **In character.** Every response follows from who that person is and what they know. If you can't point to the doc line or the channel it rests on, it doesn't happen.
- **Proportionate.** A quiet scene makes small ripples or none. Most scenes make between zero and three. "Nothing moved" is a fine result.
- **No manufactured trouble.** A ripple is a consequence, not a complication added to make things interesting. If the honest consequence is dull, it's dull.
- **No manufactured mercy either.** If the honest consequence is that the watch now has the character's description, they have it.
- **Not everything is about the character.** Pressures move for their own reasons. Most of what the world does in a week would have happened without them.
- **Roll what's open.** Whether a bystander noticed, whether the rumor got as far as the capital, whether the sick man lasted the week: Core Rules §12. Don't choose the answer that's more interesting.
- **One step at a time.** A pressure moves one step per turn, or one per stretch of time its **Moves when** names. Nothing leaps to its end because the session is ending.
- **Obey the world.** Laws, travel times, prices, and knowledge channels hold off the page exactly as they do on it.
- **The character is the user's, off the page too.** Never decide what the player character did, said, thought, or worked out in time the page skipped. If a time jump needs to know what they did with it, the user's action already said, or you ask.

## 3. Apply and log

- Write each consequence where it lives, tagged **[Off S#]**: a cast doc's Now, Knows, or Changed by; World State's Secrets & Knowledge; a place's Now or Has; Items; Economy; a group's Now; Pressures (**Stands**, **Clock**, **Last moved**, and a history line); the World Bible or Timeline for anything about the world itself.
- Anything that could reach the character in the next few scenes also goes in World State (People in motion, Pressures near the page).
- New pressures go in `Pressures.md` with every field filled.
- Add an entry to `GM Only/State/World Log.md`: what moved and why, one line each.

**How it reaches the page.** A consequence shows up when the character would actually meet it: the innkeeper is cooler, the price has gone up, there are riders on the road. It's written as what the POV sees, with no explanation of the cause they don't know (Craft Rules, "The living world doesn't leak"). People who would come looking for the character come looking. People who wouldn't, don't, however good a scene it would make.

When a later session shows an **[Off S#]** fact on the page, retag it **[S#]**.

## The time pass

When a day or more goes by (a journey, a wait, a long rest), walk the whole world for that stretch, not just what was on the page:

1. **The calendar.** What on the Story Clock's Coming up happened?
2. **Bodies.** Everyone's injuries heal or worsen on their clocks (Core Rules §8), the character's included: update the sheet. Illnesses run their course. Altered states are long gone.
3. **Money.** Wages come in, lodging and debts come due, supplies run down. Take the character's living costs off their sheet, by the Economy.
4. **Things.** Food spoils, fuel runs out, what was broken stays broken unless someone would fix it, what was left somewhere is still there or was found by someone who would find it.
5. **People.** For everyone in World State and anyone tied to a loose end or a pressure: what were they doing? Most people just live their lives; say so briefly.
6. **Pressures.** Each one moves by its **Moves when** line.
7. **Places and groups.** Seasons, business, building, politics.
8. **Knowledge.** News that was traveling has arrived.

Keep it proportionate: three quiet days is a short list. If something would interrupt the stretch (the character would be found, the storm would hit on the second day), time stops there, and the beat does too.
