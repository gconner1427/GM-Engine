# Craft Rules History

Why each rule in `Craft Rules.md` exists. Don't load this during play; open it when adding or changing a rule, to see whether the pattern has come up before.

## Where the rules came from

- **§3 to §9** were carried over from the Living World Engine's `House Rules/Craft Rules.md` when this engine was built (2026-10-07), reworded for third person limited and for beats. The reasons behind them are in that engine's `Craft Rules History.md`. Rules that only made sense for chapters, outlines, or the audit scripts were left behind.
- **§1 (The game stays off the page)** and **§2 (The player's character)** were written for this engine from what the user asked of it: no numbers in the prose, and a GM who never plays the user's character.
- A few rules were added for play that the novels never needed: "People don't hand over what the character came for," "Nobody exists for the character," "Names come from the pools," "A beat is as long as what happened," "Beats join into one continuous narrative," "No endings on a beat," "Time passes in narration."

## Corrections

One entry per rule added or changed because of a correction in play (Procedures/Corrections.md), newest first.

```
### Rule name (new | changed), date, narrative and session
**Flagged:** what the user said, in their words
**Was:** the passage as written
**Became:** the rewrite
**Why:** the pattern behind it, in a line
```

### Never "a beat" for a pause (new), 2026-10-08, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 3
**Flagged:** "A beat should never be used to show a pause"
**Was:** "'...the way you did in the crypt?' A beat, and then lower still, and quicker."
**Became:** "'...the way you did in the crypt?' She swallowed. The next part came lower still, and quicker."
**Why:** "a beat" is a stage direction that names a pause instead of showing what fills it. No other use in the session.

### Repeats in a short span: a reaction formula is spent after one use, across sessions (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-08, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 3
**Flagged:** "This exact line was used already Azalais blinked. Whatever she'd braced for, it hadn't been that one"
**Was:** "Azalais blinked. Whatever she'd braced for, it hadn't been that one, and some of the stiffness went out of her shoulders."
**Became:** "Azalais's shoulders came down an inch. Her hands stopped gripping each other in her lap."
**Why:** the same stock surprise beat had been used on the same person in Session 2. The rule only looked back "a few pages," so a repeat from the session before got through. It was also a guess at what was in her head.

### The POV only knows what they've learned: a look or a sniff doesn't itemize (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-08, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 3
**Flagged:** "When she sees the food after opening the lid she can't know what everything is inside like every ingredient that is not possible"
**Was:** "a brown bowl of something thin and steaming with barley swollen fat at the bottom of it. She bent over it and sniffed. Onion. Some kind of meat. Something green."
**Became:** "a brown bowl of something thin and steaming, with pale grains swollen fat at the bottom and soft bits floating in it that could have been anything. She bent over it and sniffed."
**Why:** the GM knows the recipe and handed it to the POV through one glance and one sniff. The existing rule covered facts from the docs but not a contents list read off a thing by the senses. Fixed alongside it: "the meat was a kind she couldn't name" became "chewy shreds in it she decided not to think too hard about."

### Physical plausibility: things happen in the order a body has to do them (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-07, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 2
**Flagged:** "when she takes her jeans off she would have to take off her shoes first before she could do her pants"
**Was:** "The jeans were the worst of it. Wet denim didn't come off, it had to be peeled... and she had to stand on one cuff to get the other foot free. Sneakers, socks, everything else."
**Became:** "She heeled her sneakers off, one against the other, and stripped the socks after them. The jeans were the worst of it... Then everything else."
**Why:** the list of clothes was ordered for rhythm, not for how a person undresses. Physical plausibility covered objects and devices but said nothing about the order of a physical sequence.

### Line of sight: a voice they can't see is a guess (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-07, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 1
**Flagged:** "Above for the woman in grey should be it sounded like the woman in grey she can't know for certain"
**Was:** "'Above!' The woman in grey, close under her, not even winded."
**Became:** "'Above!' It sounded like the woman in grey, close under her, not even winded."
**Why:** the POV was running with her back to the speaker and had heard that voice a handful of times. Naming an unseen speaker as fact is the narrator knowing, not the POV.

### No stock phrases: "a real one" explains instead of showing (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-07, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 1
**Flagged:** "When someone laughs saying a real one is explaining a laugh instead of showing the laugh"
**Was:** "the big man in armor barked a laugh into his cloth, a real one, and had to turn it into a cough."
**Became:** "the big man in armor barked a laugh into his cloth, loud enough to ring off the stone, and bent it into a cough."
**Why:** tagging a reaction "real" or "genuine" labels what kind it was instead of showing it. The existing line about "actual" and "real" only covered contrast with an earlier version. Swept in the same session: "the smile was tired and seemed real" became "a tired smile that creased his whole face."

### No "the way" to explain how something was done (changed, from "'The way' only when it earns it"), 2026-10-07, The Twilight of Kings, Session 1
**Flagged:** "looked her over the way, that type of phrase is lazy explanatory writing the way tells someone something instead of showing them"
**Was:** "He looked her over the way anyone on a road looked over anyone else, pack and quiver and boots, without much hurry about it and without anything in it she needed to mind."
**Became:** "His eyes went to her pack, the quiver, her boots, and came back up to her face."
**Why:** "the way" (and "like" or "as if" doing the same job) labels the kind of action for the reader instead of showing the action. The old rule was too loose to stop it. Swept in the same session: "He said it like a price he'd been hoping to haggle down" became "His shoulders came down under the wet coat."

### No "longer than she needed to" (new), 2026-10-08, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 5
**Flagged:** "longer than she needed to is becoming a crutch phrase it is used a lot"
**Was:** "There was an iron ring on this side, cold, and she stood there holding it longer than she needed to. Then she turned it."
**Became:** "There was an iron ring on this side, cold enough to ache in her fingers. She turned it."
**Why:** measuring a hesitation against how long it should have taken labels it as meaningful instead of showing it, and the phrase had become a stamp: it or a close cousin ("a second longer," "a moment too long") was already in Sessions 1, 2, and 3. Only the Session 5 use was changed; earlier sessions left alone.
