# Craft Rules History

Why each rule in `Craft Rules.md` exists. Don't load this during play; open it when adding or changing a rule, to see whether the pattern has come up before.

## Where the rules came from

- **§3 to §9** were carried over from the Living World Engine's `House Rules/Craft Rules.md` when this engine was built (2026-10-07), reworded for third person limited and for beats. The reasons behind them are in that engine's `Craft Rules History.md`. Rules that only made sense for chapters, outlines, or the audit scripts were left behind.
- **§1 (The game stays off the page)** and **§2 (The player's character)** were written for this engine from what the user asked of it: no numbers in the prose, and a GM who never plays the user's character.
- A few rules were added for play that the novels never needed: "People don't hand over what the character came for," "Nobody exists for the character," "Names come from the pools," "A beat is as long as what happened," "Beats join into one continuous narrative," "No endings on a beat," "Time passes in narration."

## Corrections

One entry per rule added or changed because of a correction in play (Procedures/Corrections.md), newest first.

```
### Rule name (new | changed), date, narrative and session
**Flagged:** what the user said, in their words
**Was:** the passage as written
**Became:** the rewrite
**Why:** the pattern behind it, in a line
```

### Line of sight: a voice they can't see is a guess (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-07, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 1
**Flagged:** "Above for the woman in grey should be it sounded like the woman in grey she can't know for certain"
**Was:** "'Above!' The woman in grey, close under her, not even winded."
**Became:** "'Above!' It sounded like the woman in grey, close under her, not even winded."
**Why:** the POV was running with her back to the speaker and had heard that voice a handful of times. Naming an unseen speaker as fact is the narrator knowing, not the POV.

### No stock phrases: "a real one" explains instead of showing (changed, sharpened), 2026-10-07, The Traveler from the Other Shore, Session 1
**Flagged:** "When someone laughs saying a real one is explaining a laugh instead of showing the laugh"
**Was:** "the big man in armor barked a laugh into his cloth, a real one, and had to turn it into a cough."
**Became:** "the big man in armor barked a laugh into his cloth, loud enough to ring off the stone, and bent it into a cough."
**Why:** tagging a reaction "real" or "genuine" labels what kind it was instead of showing it. The existing line about "actual" and "real" only covered contrast with an earlier version. Swept in the same session: "the smile was tired and seemed real" became "a tired smile that creased his whole face."

### No "the way" to explain how something was done (changed, from "'The way' only when it earns it"), 2026-10-07, The Twilight of Kings, Session 1
**Flagged:** "looked her over the way, that type of phrase is lazy explanatory writing the way tells someone something instead of showing them"
**Was:** "He looked her over the way anyone on a road looked over anyone else, pack and quiver and boots, without much hurry about it and without anything in it she needed to mind."
**Became:** "His eyes went to her pack, the quiver, her boots, and came back up to her face."
**Why:** "the way" (and "like" or "as if" doing the same job) labels the kind of action for the reader instead of showing the action. The old rule was too loose to stop it. Swept in the same session: "He said it like a price he'd been hoping to haggle down" became "His shoulders came down under the wet coat."
