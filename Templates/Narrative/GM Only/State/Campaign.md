# Campaign

What {{NARRATIVE}} is about: the goal the character is meant to work toward, what stands against it, and what put them in it. You prepare it the way a GM prepares a campaign, before the first session, and the world is built around it (Procedures/New Narrative.md, step 3). Read it whole at the start of every conversation.

It's an end in mind and a road toward it, never a script. You decide what the campaign is; the player and the dice decide how it goes (GM Rules, "The campaign").

**Everything here is GM knowledge.** The character knows the campaign only as far as the Journal's "The goal" says.

Tags as everywhere: **[Setup]** what the player gave, **[GM]** yours and not on the page yet, **[S#]** on the page in that session.

## The goal

What the character is meant to do, in a line: bring down the dark lord, end the war, find the missing sister. Then what would count as done, plainly enough that you'll know it when it happens.

## What's at stake

What happens if nobody stops it, to whom, and by when. This is the campaign's pressure in `Pressures.md` (name it here): it has a clock and it moves whether or not the character does.

## The opposition

Who or what stands between the character and the goal, by their doc names: what they want, why, and what they'll do when someone gets in the way. People with reasons of their own, never an obstacle put there for the character.

Unknown.

## The hook

Why this character is in it. Written once the character is made (Procedures/New Narrative.md, step 6), from who the player made:

- **What brings it to them:** the event that opens the first session.
- **Why it's theirs:** what on their sheet it catches (someone they love, something they want, a debt, their trade, where they're from), so they have a reason of their own to go after the goal.
- **What they know at the start:** how much of the goal the hook shows them. Usually the first step, not the whole.
- **Built on:** what the player gave that the hook and the opening stand on (a want, a trade, a home, something they said in braces), tagged **[Setup]**. The opening itself is yours and is never put to the player.

Unknown.

## The road

Three to five stages between the hook and the end. Each is something that has to be learned, won, reached, or decided before the goal can be had, with where it is and who's in the way. They're milestones, never scenes: there are several ways through each, and the character may find one you didn't think of, skip one, or take them out of order. Write the next stage in full and the later ones in a line each, and fill each in as play comes near it.

## How it could end

Two or three ways it could honestly come out: the goal reached, the opposition winning, and at least one that's neither. You don't choose among them and you don't protect any of them.

Unknown.

## Threads

The ways the campaign can reach the character from wherever they are: who would come looking for them, what news would travel, what they're carrying that someone wants, who they've promised. When you open a session (Procedures/Play.md, "Opening a session"), this is the first place to look. Add one whenever play makes one, and strike those that are spent.

## Where it stands

Brought up to date at the end of every session.

- **Stage:** (which stage of the road the character is in or nearest to)
- **They know:** (how much of the goal, the stakes, and the opposition the character has learned)
- **They have:** (allies, means, and leads toward the goal)
- **Last put in front of them:** (the last time the campaign crossed their path, and what they did about it) [S#]
